   - HNWIs want simplicity, not complexity
   - Can expand to 5-6 in v2.1 if demand exists

## 3. Default Weighting Research

| Template | BTC | ETH | SOL | USDC | Character |
|----------|-----|-----|-----|------|-----------|
| A: BTC Anchor | 50% | 25% | 5% | 20% | Conservative |
| B: Balanced | 40% | 30% | 10% | 20% | Moderate |

### Analysis

**Template A (BTC Anchor):**
- Pros: Maximum "digital gold" exposure, lowest volatility
- Cons: Underweights ETH ecosystem, SOL almost negligible
- Fit: Ultra-conservative HNWI

**Template B (Balanced):**
- Pros: Meaningful positions in all assets, 20% buffer
- Cons: None significant
- Fit: Most HNWIs who want diversified crypto exposure

**Template C (Platform Tilt):**
- Pros: Equal weighting to BTC/ETH reflects "two pillars" view
- Cons: May not align with HNWI preference for BTC dominance
- Fit: ETH-bullish investors

**Template D (Growth):**
- Pros: Maximum exposure to higher-beta assets
- Cons: 20% SOL is high for unproven asset, more volatile
- Fit: Risk-tolerant HNWI

### Decision: Template B (40/30/10/20)

**Rationale:**

1. **BTC at 40%:** Clear anchor position. HNWIs seeking "digital gold" get meaningful exposure. Not so dominant that other assets are irrelevant.

2. **ETH at 30%:** Second pillar. Institutional consensus position. Meaningful but not equal to BTC (respects market cap hierarchy).

3. **SOL at 10%:** Growth exposure without overcommitment. If SOL outperforms, this position will grow naturally. If it underperforms, damage is contained.

4. **USDC at 20%:** Substantial buffer. Provides:
   - ~20% reduction in portfolio volatility
   - Ammunition for buying dips
   - Psychological stability during drawdowns
   - Higher than some allocations (15%) because HNWIs value stability

**Why This is the "Continue Without Thinking" Option:**

An HNWI who knows nothing about crypto portfolio construction but trusts Meezan should be able to accept this default and have a sensible, institutional-grade allocation. It's not optimized for maximum returns — it's optimized for being reasonable in most scenarios.

## 4. Deviation (Drift) Research

### Cost-Benefit Analysis

| Threshold | Rebalance Frequency | Gas Cost/Year | Slippage Impact | Tax Events |
|-----------|---------------------|---------------|-----------------|------------|
| 2% | Weekly+ | High | Higher | Many |
| 3% | 2-3x/month | Medium | Medium | Moderate |
| **5%** | **Monthly** | **Low** | **Low** | **Few** |
| 8% | Quarterly | Very low | Very low | Minimal |
| 15% | Rarely | Minimal | Minimal | Rare |

### Behavioural Factors

**Too Tight (2%):**
- Creates anxiety (constant activity notifications)
- Feels like trading (HNWIs want "set and forget")
- Higher costs erode returns

**Too Loose (15%+):**
- Defeats purpose of systematic rebalancing
- Portfolio can drift significantly (e.g., 40% BTC → 55% BTC)
- Loses discipline benefit

**Sweet Spot (5%):**
- Triggers during meaningful market moves
- Maintains target discipline without over-trading
- Typical frequency: Monthly in volatile markets, quarterly in calm markets

### Decision: 5% Default

**With alternatives:**

| Preset | Threshold | Use Case |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| Tight | 3% | Strict discipline, accept more activity |
| **Default** | **5%** | **Balanced discipline and efficiency** |
| Relaxed | 8% | Minimal activity, accept more drift |

**Rationale:**

5% drift means: "If any asset is 5 percentage points off target, rebalance."

For the 40/30/10/20 portfolio:
- BTC drifts from 40% to 45% → rebalance triggers
- ETH drifts from 30% to 35% → rebalance triggers
- SOL drifts from 10% to 15% → rebalance triggers

This catches meaningful moves without triggering on noise.

## 5. Behaviour Under Stress

### Scenario 1: Sharp Rally (BTC +50% in 30 days)

**What happens:**
```
Before rally:  BTC 40% | ETH 30% | SOL 10% | USDC 20%
After rally:   BTC 52% | ETH 26% | SOL 8%  | USDC 14%
BTC drift: 12% → Threshold exceeded
```

**System response:**
1. Detects BTC 12% overweight
2. Calculates rebalance: Sell ~$X of BTC → Buy ETH, SOL, USDC
3. Executes: BTC → USDC → ETH, USDC → USDC (held), USDC → SOL
4. Result: Portfolio returns to 40/30/10/20

**HNWI benefit:**
- Automatically "sells the rip"
- Locks in gains without emotional decision
- Prevents portfolio from becoming BTC-only over time

### Scenario 2: Sharp Drawdown (All crypto -40%)

**What happens:**
```
Before crash:  BTC 40% | ETH 30% | SOL 10% | USDC 20%
After crash:   BTC 29% | ETH 22% | SOL 7%  | USDC 42%
USDC drift: 22% → Threshold exceeded (USDC weight increased because crypto values fell)
```

**System response:**
1. Detects USDC 22% overweight (crypto underweight)
2. Calculates rebalance: Sell USDC → Buy BTC, ETH, SOL
3. Executes: USDC → BTC, USDC → ETH, USDC → SOL
4. Result: Portfolio returns to 40/30/10/20

**HNWI benefit:**
- Automatically "buys the dip"
- Forced buying discipline when others panic
- USDC buffer serves its purpose: ammunition for drawdowns

### Scenario 3: Mixed Movement (BTC +30%, SOL -40%, ETH flat)

**What happens:**
```
Before:        BTC 40% | ETH 30% | SOL 10% | USDC 20%
After:         BTC 48% | ETH 28% | SOL 5%  | USDC 19%
BTC drift: 8% → Threshold exceeded
SOL drift: 5% → Also exceeded
```

**System response:**
1. Detects multiple assets off-target
2. Calculates: Sell BTC excess → Buy SOL deficit (via USDC)
3. Executes multi-swap rebalance
4. Result: Portfolio returns to target

**HNWI benefit:**
- Sells winner (BTC), buys loser (SOL)
- Classic rebalancing: captures mean reversion
- No need to pick which asset will recover — system maintains all targets

### Why This Benefits Long-Term Accumulation

1. **Removes emotion:** Human instinct is to hold winners and sell losers. Rebalancing does the opposite, which is mathematically correct for long-term.

2. **Buys low, sells high automatically:** Rebalancing forces selling after rallies and buying after drawdowns.

3. **Maintains risk profile:** Without rebalancing, a portfolio that starts 40/30/10/20 could become 70/20/5/5 after a BTC rally. That's not what the user signed up for.

4. **Compounds over time:** Studies show systematic rebalancing adds 0.5-1% annual return vs. buy-and-hold, with lower volatility.
